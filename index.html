07  /  Questions

The short answers

What does ad management cost?

It depends on the plan you pick and what you need. We work that out on the call, and you'll know the number before you commit to anything.

Do I need to be on both Google and Meta?

Usually no, not at the start. Most local businesses do better putting the full budget behind one platform first. We pick the right one on the call.

How much ad budget do I need?

This is the money that goes to Google or Meta, not to me. For Meta, plan on about $1,500 a month at the low end, with most spending $2,000 to $5,000. For Google Search the floor is closer to $2,000 a month, and most spend $3,000 to $8,000. Below those numbers the campaign never gathers enough data to improve.

How fast will I see results?

Campaigns need a couple of weeks to gather data before they settle. Most clients see steady lead flow inside the first month, then it improves as we test and tighten.

What if I already have a website?

Then we use it. If it's holding the ads back, I'll tell you straight and we fix that first.

How do the plans work?

Ad management runs on a set term: 3, 6, or 12 months. A campaign needs time to gather data, settle, and improve, so 3 months is the shortest that gives the work a fair shot.

Who actually does the work?

I do. You're not getting passed to a junior or an offshore team. One person, accountable, hands on the account.

How a paid ad budget works

The advertising budget is separate from the management fee and goes directly to Google or Meta. For Meta, a workable starting point is around $1,500 a month, with most active accounts spending between $2,000 and $5,000. For Google Search, the floor sits closer to $2,000 a month, with most spending $3,000 to $8,000. Below those thresholds, a campaign rarely gathers enough conversion data to improve on its own, which is part of why campaigns are run on a minimum three-month term. Details are in the frequently asked questions.
